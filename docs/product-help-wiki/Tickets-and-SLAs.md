# Tickets and SLAs

Tickets track customer service and support work. SLAs define expected response or resolution timing.

## Ticket Basics

A useful ticket has:

- Subject
- Description
- Customer or contact
- Priority
- Status
- Owner
- Due dates or SLA context when applicable

Keep the subject concise. Put details in the description, comments, or attachments.

## Status Flow

Common status meanings:

- Open: submitted and not yet actively handled.
- In progress: someone is working on it.
- Pending: waiting on the customer or an external dependency.
- Resolved: solution provided.
- Closed: no further work expected.

Use statuses consistently. Reporting depends on it.

## Priority

Priority should describe business urgency, not customer volume.

Use high priority when the impact is significant, time-sensitive, or blocks important work.

## SLAs

SLA policies help enforce support commitments. Admins define policies; agents work tickets with those expectations visible.

When an SLA warning appears, update the ticket immediately:

- Assign owner.
- Add a clear internal note.
- Respond to the customer if needed.
- Escalate if the work cannot be completed in time.

## Ticket Deflection

When customers or agents create tickets, relevant Knowledge Base articles may help avoid duplicate support work. These articles come from your tenant KB, not Omnir product help.
